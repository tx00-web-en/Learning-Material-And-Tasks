# Agenda for Team Meeting

## 1. Team Check-in & Team Development — Tuckman's Model

Now that your team has worked together for a week, take **10–15 minutes** to reflect on your collaboration and working habits.

### Where is our team right now?

Discuss which stage best describes your team:

* **Forming:** Still getting to know each other and clarifying expectations.
* **Storming:** Experiencing friction, differing opinions, or confusion over tasks and technical choices.
* **Norming:** Developing shared agreements, clear communication habits, and smoother collaboration.
* **Performing:** Working autonomously, resolving issues quickly, and delivering predictably.

Your team does not need to agree on one stage immediately. Discuss your reasoning.

### Ways of Working Check

Discuss:

* Are our communication channels working well?
* Is the **Daily Scrum** helping us inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and identify what needs attention?
* Is everyone contributing to the Sprint, while recognizing that contribution does not necessarily mean doing identical tasks?
* Is everyone getting support when they are stuck?
* Are responsibilities clear?
* What is one agreement we can establish today to make our team stronger?

### Clarify Team Responsibilities

Make sure everyone knows who is currently taking responsibility for areas such as:

* Product Backlog / Product Owner responsibilities
* Prototype / UX
* Frontend / CSS
* AI use and AI-related tasks
* Technical implementation
* Documentation
* Presentation / demo

**Important:** Responsibilities can be shared and can change. Scrum does not require permanent ownership of individual technical areas.

# 2. Backlog Health Check — Applying DEEP & INVEST

Open your team's **Trello board** and conduct a focused **Product Backlog refinement and health check**.

The purpose is not to make every backlog item perfect.

The purpose is to make the backlog **understandable, appropriately refined, estimated, emergent, and meaningfully ordered**.

---

## Zoom Out: Is Our Product Backlog DEEP?

### D — Detailed appropriately

Ask:

* Are near-term stories sufficiently refined?
* Do important near-term stories have clear acceptance criteria or another clear way to determine completion?
* Are future ideas still appropriately high-level?
* Have we avoided spending too much time specifying work that we may not do for several Sprints?

### E — Estimated

Ask:

* Have we assigned Story Points to the items that are sufficiently understood to estimate?
* Are our estimates based on **relative sizing**?
* Are there items with estimates that we do not actually understand?
* Do any items need refinement before they can be estimated confidently?

### E — Emergent

Ask:

* What did we learn this week?
* Has anything changed because of user feedback, research, technical discoveries, or team discussions?
* Should we **add, remove, split, rewrite, or modify** any backlog items?
* Have any priorities changed because of what we learned?

### P — Prioritized

Ask:

* Is the Product Backlog meaningfully ordered?
* Can we explain why an item is above another item?
* Are we avoiding a situation where everything is simply marked **High Priority**?
* Does the ordering reflect value, learning, risk, dependencies, and the current product direction?

**Remember:** A sequential 1–2–3 ranking can be useful for this exercise, but Scrum requires an **ordered Product Backlog**, not necessarily a numbered list.

---

## Zoom In: Are Our Individual Stories INVEST?

Pick **2–3 important user stories** from your board and check them carefully.

### I — Independent

* Can this story reasonably be developed without being heavily blocked by another story?
* Are there dependencies that should be reduced or made explicit?

### N — Negotiable

* Is the story open to discussion?
* Have we avoided turning the story into a rigid technical specification?

### V — Valuable

* Is the customer, user, or stakeholder value clear?
* Does the story describe an outcome rather than simply a technical activity?

### E — Estimable

* Does the team understand the story well enough to estimate it?
* Are there major unknowns that need clarification?

### S — Small

* Is the story small enough to be completed within a Sprint?
* If it is too large, can we split it into smaller valuable pieces?

### T — Testable

* Can we determine whether the story has been successfully completed?
* Are the acceptance criteria clear enough?
* Where appropriate, could we express acceptance criteria using **Given / When / Then**?

### After the review

Make actual changes to your Trello board.

Do not only discuss problems.

You might:

* rewrite a story,
* split a story,
* add acceptance criteria,
* change an estimate,
* change the order,
* remove an unnecessary item,
* add a new item,
* clarify the user value,
* identify a dependency.

# 3. Finalize the Sprint 1 Prototype

Review the current state of your prototype in **Figma, Draw.io, or your chosen tool**.

Ensure that:

* The **core user flow** is represented from beginning to end.
* The prototype reflects the most important user stories in your Sprint 1 work.
* The main screens/interactions needed to demonstrate the concept are present.
* The prototype is coherent enough to demonstrate to stakeholders/classmates next week.
* The team can explain what the prototype demonstrates and what is **not** implemented.
* Any important assumptions or limitations are documented.

### Important

Your prototype does **not** need to be a production-ready application.

You should be able to demonstrate the **intended product experience and the learning achieved during Sprint 1**.
