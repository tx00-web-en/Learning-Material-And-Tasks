# Scrum Warm-Up Activity: Reverse Engineering Airbnb

## Objective

The goal of this activity is to make Scrum terminology concrete by exploring an existing digital product and identifying the **roles, user stories, backlog items, sprint backlog, and Scrum ceremonies** involved in developing it.

You will work in teams and use **Airbnb** as an example of an existing product.

You are **not** expected to design or build the complete application. The purpose of this activity is to understand how Scrum concepts can be applied to a real product.

You may use AI tools such as ChatGPT or other AI tools to help you explore the application, generate ideas, or challenge your assumptions. 

- https://chatgpt.com/

---

# Task 1 — Reverse Engineer Airbnb

Visit Airbnb:

**https://www.airbnb.com/**

Spend approximately **10–15 minutes exploring the website**.

Explore the application from the perspective of a user who wants to find and book accommodation.

Think about questions such as:

* What can a user do?
* What information does the user need?
* What are the main steps in the user journey?
* What different types of users are involved?
* What happens before, during, and after a booking?
* What features are essential?
* What features are useful but not essential?

You do not need to explore every feature. Focus on identifying the main functionality of the product.

---

# Task 2 — Identify the Users and Roles

Based on your exploration, identify the main types of users who interact with Airbnb.

For example:

* Guest
* Host
* Admin

Consider:

* What does each user want to accomplish?
* What does each user need from the system?
* How are their needs different?

Then identify **one or two important goals** for each user type.

---

# Task 3 — Discover User Stories

Based on your exploration, create **at least 8 user stories**.

Use the following format:

> **As a [user], I need [function], so that [benefit].**

For example:

> As a guest, I need to search for accommodation by location and dates, so that I can find places that are available for my trip.

> As a guest, I need to view the details of an accommodation, so that I can decide whether it is suitable for me.

> As a host, I need to create a listing, so that I can offer my property to potential guests.

Try to write stories from the **user's perspective**, rather than simply describing interface elements.

For example:

**Less useful:**

> As a guest, I need a search button.

**Better:**

> As a guest, I need to search for accommodation by location and dates, so that I can find a suitable place to stay.

---

# Task 4 — Create a Product Backlog

Combine your user stories into a **Product Backlog**.

Add a priority to each story.

You can use:

* High
* Medium
* Low

or another prioritization method agreed upon by your team.

Now imagine that you are responsible for deciding what should be developed first.

Discuss:

* Which features provide the most value?
* Which features are essential for the first version?
* Which features could wait?
* What would you consider the **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)**?

Be prepared to explain your prioritization decisions.

> Note that product backlog encompasses all features, user stories, bug fixes, technical tasks, and any other work that contributes to the product's development. [More here](./scrum.md#product-backlog-vs-user-stories)

---

# Task 5 — Who Does What?

Now connect your activity to Scrum roles.

Imagine that your team is developing an Airbnb-like application.

Discuss the responsibilities of the following Scrum roles:

### Product Owner

* What decisions would the Product Owner make?
* Who decides which backlog items are more important?
* Who represents the needs of users and stakeholders?

### Scrum Master

* What would the Scrum Master do?
* Would the Scrum Master tell the Developers what to build?
* What problems might the Scrum Master help the team address?

### Developers

* What is the responsibility of the Developers?
* Who decides how the selected functionality will be implemented?

Do not worry about memorizing formal definitions yet. Base your answers on the situation you have just worked with.

---

# Task 6 — Plan a Sprint

Imagine that your team is about to start a Sprint.

You cannot develop everything in the Product Backlog.

Select a small number of user stories that you believe should be included in the **first Sprint**.

These selected items form your:

> **Sprint Backlog**

Now define a **Sprint Goal**.

For example:

> "Enable a guest to find suitable accommodation and view the details of a property."

Discuss:

* Why did you select these stories?
* Why did you leave other stories out?
* How does your Sprint Goal relate to the selected stories?

---

# Task 7 — Break Stories into Tasks

Choose **2–3 user stories** from your Sprint Backlog.

Break each story into smaller tasks that the Developers might need to complete.

For example:

### User Story

> As a guest, I need to search for accommodation by location and dates, so that I can find a suitable place to stay.

### Possible tasks

* Design the search interface
* Create the location input
* Add date selection
* Create the search functionality
* Retrieve available properties
* Display search results
* Test the search flow

Discuss:

> What is the difference between a **user story** and a **task**?

---

# Task 8 — Scrum Ceremonies

Now imagine that your team is going to work on the Sprint you have just planned.

Discuss what would happen during each of these Scrum events.

### Sprint Planning

What would the team do before starting the Sprint?

### Daily Scrum

Imagine that the Sprint lasts two weeks.

What would the Developers discuss during a Daily Scrum?

What is the purpose of the Daily Scrum?

### Sprint Review

At the end of the Sprint, what would the team show to stakeholders?

What kind of feedback might they receive?

---

# Task 9 — Product Backlog vs Sprint Backlog

Look at the work you created in the previous tasks.

Discuss:

**Product Backlog**

* What does it contain?
* Who is responsible for ordering it?
* Does it contain everything the product might need?

**Sprint Backlog**

* How is it different?
* Who creates it?
* Why doesn't it contain everything from the Product Backlog?

Be ready to explain the difference in your own words.

---

# Task 10 — Burndown Chart

Imagine that your Sprint lasts **10 working days**.

The team starts with **20 story points** in the Sprint Backlog.

Discuss:

* What would a burndown chart show?
* What would you expect to happen to the remaining work as the Sprint progresses?
* What might it mean if the remaining work is not decreasing?
* What might it mean if the team finishes much earlier than expected?

You do not need to create a perfect chart. Focus on understanding what the chart is intended to communicate.

---

# Final Discussion

As a team, discuss the following:

1. Which Scrum concept became clearer after doing this activity?
2. Which concept is still unclear?
3. What is the difference between a **role**, an **artifact**, and a **ceremony/event**?
4. Why might Scrum use a Product Backlog rather than simply giving Developers a list of features?
5. Why is prioritization important?
6. What could happen if the team tried to build everything at once?

## AI Use

You may use AI tools such as ChatGPT or other AI assistants during the activity.

AI can help you:

* identify possible user types
* generate candidate user stories
* suggest missing functionality
* challenge your prioritization
* explain Scrum terminology
* give alternative perspectives

However, **your team is responsible for the final decisions**.

Do not simply copy an AI-generated Product Backlog. Discuss the suggestions and decide what makes sense for your product.

## Important

This is a **warm-up activity**.

You are not expected to become experts in Scrum during this exercise.

The purpose is to make the Scrum concepts we will use later **concrete and familiar**.

Later today, you will apply these concepts to your own project.
