# Activity: Scrum Metrics Showdown

**Assessment:** Group competition

**Winning group bonus:** +1 bonus mark (equivalent to 5 points) for each member.

---

## 1. Activity overview

The Scrum Metrics Showdown is a team-based activity focused on understanding, interpreting, and defending Scrum metrics.

The activity consists of two phases:

1. **Team investigation:** Analyze a fictional Scrum team's situation, select appropriate metrics, and prepare a recommendation.
2. **Friendly challenge:** Split into pairs and challenge another group's interpretation and recommendation.

The objective is not simply to identify the correct metric. The objective is to explain why a metric is useful, interpret evidence accurately, recognize limitations, and defend a recommendation.

### Group structure

Each group consists of four members.

Before the competition, all four members must understand the scenario, the metrics, and the group's recommendation.

After preparation, the group splits into two pairs:

* Pair A: Members 1 and 2.
* Pair B: Members 3 and 4.

Two pairs from one group meet two pairs from another group. Each pair participates in both defending its own group's recommendation and challenging the other group's recommendation.

All four members contribute to the group's final score.

---

## 2. Learning objectives

By the end of the activity, the following should be demonstrated:

* Understanding of the main Scrum metrics.
* Ability to calculate and interpret basic Scrum metrics.
* Ability to select appropriate metrics for a specific situation.
* Ability to explain the limitations of metrics.
* Ability to challenge another group's reasoning using evidence.
* Ability to adapt a recommendation when new information appears.
* Understanding of the difference between measuring activity, delivery, flow, and value.

---

## 3. Preparation: Understanding Scrum metrics

Before starting the investigation, review the main Scrum metrics.

The following resources can be used:

1. [Atlassian — Scrum Metrics](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/scrum/scrum-metrics)
2. [ITPhobia — 11 Scrum Metrics and Their Value](https://itphobia.com/11-scrum-metrics-and-their-value-to-scrum-teams/)
3. [Dee Project Manager — Scrum Metrics](https://deeprojectmanager.com/scrum-metrics/)
4. [Agile Scrum Sprint Metrics Template](https://github.com/eric-spink-agile/agile-scrum-sprint-metrics-template)

### AI use

AI tools may be used during the preparation and learning phase to understand Scrum metrics.

Examples of appropriate use:

* Ask for a simple explanation of velocity.
* Ask for an example of a burndown chart.
* Compare velocity, throughput, and cycle time.
* Ask for help understanding a calculation.
* Practice answering questions about Scrum metrics.
* Ask for feedback on an initial interpretation.

AI may support understanding, but the final decisions must be discussed and understood by all four group members.

During the challenge, AI should not be used to generate live answers. Prepared notes and calculations may be used.

**The objective is to demonstrate understanding, not simply to produce an answer.**

# 4. Fictional scenario: AppNest

AppNest is a fictional software company developing a mobile application for university students.

The Scrum Team consists of five developers and works in two-week Sprints.

The Product Owner has raised concerns about the team's performance.

> "Management is unhappy. Our velocity is not increasing, and customers are complaining that important features are missing. We need to improve our performance."

The Scrum Master has collected the following information.

## Sprint data

| Sprint | Planned points | Completed points | Stories completed |
| ------ | -------------: | ---------------: | ----------------: |
| 1      |             20 |               18 |                 6 |
| 2      |             25 |               20 |                 7 |
| 3      |             30 |               24 |                 9 |
| 4      |             35 |               20 |                 8 |

### Additional information

1. During Sprint 4, one developer was sick for most of the Sprint.
2. The Product Owner added 10 points of new work during Sprint 4.
3. Three of the eight completed stories in Sprint 4 were bug fixes.
4. Customers complain that the application is slow.
5. Management wants the team to increase velocity from 20 to 50.
6. Many stories are waiting for review.

## Part B — Interpret the data

Discuss the following questions.

### Question 1

Is AppNest's velocity improving, declining, or relatively stable?

Calculate the average velocity across the four Sprints.

$$
\text{Average velocity} =
\frac{\text{Total completed points}}{\text{Number of Sprints}}
$$

### Question 2

Does the velocity of 20 points in Sprint 4 mean that the team performed badly?

Explain your reasoning using the available information.

### Question 3

What additional information would be useful before making a decision?

Consider the following:

* Team capacity.
* Sprint Goal achievement.
* Work waiting for review.
* Quality and defects.
* Customer feedback.
* Scope changes.

### Question 4

What is the most important problem that AppNest should investigate?

Explain why.

## Part D — Prepare the defense

# 6. Friendly challenge

After the team investigation, split into two pairs.

## Pair structure

Group X:

* Pair X-A: Members 1 and 2.
* Pair X-B: Members 3 and 4.

Group Y:

* Pair Y-A: Members 1 and 2.
* Pair Y-B: Members 3 and 4.

### Pairing

* Pair X-A meets Pair Y-A.
* Pair X-B meets Pair Y-B.

Each pair participates in both attack and defence.

## Round format

Each pairing lasts approximately 9 minutes.

| Time      | Activity                             |
| --------- | ------------------------------------ |
| 2 minutes | Group X presents its recommendation. |
| 2 minutes | Group Y presents its recommendation. |
| 2 minutes | Group X challenges Group Y.          |
| 2 minutes | Group Y challenges Group X.          |
| 1 minute  | Score the round.                     |

### Presentation requirements

Each pair must explain:

1. The three selected metrics.
2. The reasoning behind the selection.
3. The most important finding from the data.
4. The recommendation for the next Sprint.

The presentation should be concise and evidence-based.

---

## 7. Attack questions

A challenge should be based on reasoning or evidence.

Possible questions include:

* Why did you choose velocity instead of cycle time?
* What does your chosen metric actually measure?
* What can you conclude from the data?
* What can you not conclude?
* Does the velocity drop mean that the team performed badly?
* Is this metric suitable as a performance target?
* Could this metric be manipulated?
* How does your metric help improve the team's situation?
* Why did you not choose Sprint Goal completion?
* What would happen if the Product Owner added more work next Sprint?
* Can the velocity of two different teams be compared fairly?
* What additional information would be useful?
* How would your recommendation change if the team had fewer developers?

### Challenge rule

A useful challenge explains why the other group's reasoning may be incomplete, misleading, or less appropriate for the situation.

A disagreement without explanation does not count as a strong challenge.

# 8. Scoring

Each pairing is worth a maximum of 10 points.

| Criterion                             | Points |
| ------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Understanding of metrics              |      2 |
| Interpretation of the scenario        |      2 |
| Quality of recommendation             |      2 |
| Challenge questions            |      2 |
| Evidence-based reasoning and teamwork |      2 |
| **Total per pairing**                 | **10** |

### Group score

Each group participates in two pairings.

| Pairing               |       Maximum |
| --------------------- | ------------: |
| Pair A                |     10 points |
| Pair B                |     10 points |
| **Total group score** | **20 points** |

The group with the highest total score wins.

### Bonus

Every member of the winning group receives:

**+1 bonus mark (equivalent to 5 points).**

The bonus is awarded to all four members of the winning group.

# 9. Twist round: New evidence

After the challenge round, new information is revealed.

## Management's new demand

> "From the next Sprint, AppNest must reach 50 story points. The team with the highest velocity will be considered the best team."

### Task

Discuss the following questions.

1. Should the recommendation change?
2. Which metric should management stop using as a performance target?
3. What should AppNest measure instead?
4. How should the situation be explained to management?

### Twist round timing

* 3 minutes: Discuss the new information.
* 2 minutes: Prepare the response.
* 5 minutes: Share and compare the recommendations.

The twist round can be used as a tiebreaker if two groups have the same score.

# 10. Final reflection

Discuss the following questions after the competition.

1. Which metric was the most difficult to understand?
2. Did any group choose a different metric? Why?
3. Did the challenge change the way the situation was understood?
4. What is the danger of using velocity as a target?
5. Which metric would be most useful for AppNest?
6. What is the difference between measuring activity and measuring value?
7. What makes a Scrum metric useful?

### Final takeaway

Scrum metrics are used to help a Scrum Team inspect its situation, make better decisions, and improve.

A metric should not be treated as a target simply because it is easy to measure.

The most useful metric is one that helps the team understand what is happening and decide what to do next.
